## The confusion the episode is built to undo

A defined-contribution pot is a balance. A paycheck is a flow. Turning a balance into a flow is a choice with tradeoffs, and the choice has names. In drawdown, the pot stays invested and you take withdrawals. The pot can fall because of withdrawals and because markets fall. It can also rise. Nothing in that sentence is a promise about a future year.

An annuity is a contract with an insurer. You exchange a sum for an income the contract describes. The income depends on the quote you are offered on the day, your age, and the options you tick, such as inflation protection or a continuing income for a spouse. A rate that appeared in someone else's headline is not your quote. This episode does not read a rate out as if it were current.

## The 4 percent story, kept in its box

Viewers have heard of a "4 percent rule." It comes from US historical research on withdrawal rates, often associated with William Bengen's work, using US market history and US inflation. It is a study of the past, not an entitlement, and it is not a UK tax rule. UK withdrawals can interact with income tax, the State Pension, and the shape of your other savings. This episode says that plainly and then leaves the rule in its box. It is not used as the answer.

## Three questions, each with the assumption on a card

Card one. "How long might the income need to last?" The video writes an age range as an assumption, not a prediction. It points at the Office for National Statistics life-table pages as the public place those ranges come from, and it says a table is a population picture, not your lifespan: https://www.ons.gov.uk/peoplepopulationandcommunity/birthsdeathsandmarriages/lifeexpectancies

Card two. "What income already arrives without touching the pot?" The State Pension, a defined-benefit pension, rent, or a partner's earnings change the size of the gap. The video shows a blank line for each and fills only a fictional gap labeled "example." The fictional gap is £800 a month. That number is a prop. It is not a recommendation and not a typical household.

Card three. "What happens to the pot if markets fall in the first years of withdrawals?" The video shows two paper paths for the same fictional pot of £250,000, also a prop. Path A withdraws the example gap every year while the paper return is flat. Path B withdraws the same gap in a year the paper return is minus 15 percent, then flat. The drawing exists to show sequence, the order of returns, not to forecast a crash. No path is labeled "what will happen."

## What the episode will not do

It will not say you are safe. It will not name a platform. It will not tell you to buy an annuity or to avoid one. It will not put an AI presenter on screen and call that presenter a planner. A human narrator reads the cards. If a later edit uses software to tidy a sentence, the description says so, and the narrator is still not an adviser.

## Close

Return to the jar and the bill. The pot is the jar. The paycheck is a decision you still have to design, with a quote if you want a contract and with a written assumption if you want withdrawals. The next episode changes only one card: the income that already arrives. The pot prop stays the same so the comparison is visible.
