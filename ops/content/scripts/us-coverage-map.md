## What the coverage page says

Medicare.gov describes two main ways to get Medicare coverage after Part A and Part B: Original Medicare, and Medicare Advantage. Original Medicare pays a share of covered services and you pay a share. You can see any doctor or hospital that takes Medicare in the United States. You can add drug coverage and you can add a supplement.

Medicare Advantage is a Medicare-approved plan from a private company. It is an alternative way to get Part A and Part B, and most of those plans include drug coverage. Networks apply in many cases. Extra benefits such as dental or vision depend on the plan. You keep paying the Part B premium.

The video reads those distinctions from the page. It does not add a ranking.

## Where Medigap fits, and where it does not

Medigap is extra insurance from a private company that helps pay your share under Original Medicare. Medicare.gov says a Medigap policy covers one person. It says you generally need Part A and Part B to buy one. It says Medigap generally does not cover long-term care, vision, dental, hearing aids, or prescription drugs. Some policies include foreign-travel emergency coverage up to the policy's limits.

The same pages say you do not pair Medigap with Medicare Advantage. Medigap does not pay Advantage copayments, deductibles, and premiums. If a salesperson describes Medigap as a discount card for an Advantage plan, that description does not match the page this episode is reading. The how-it-works page is https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/basics/how-medigap-works

## The letter chart, only as a chart

Medicare.gov publishes a chart of standardized letters. Benefits in a letter are the same across companies that sell that letter. Price is the part that differs. On the chart retrieved for this episode, Plan K's 2026 out-of-pocket limit is $8,000 and Plan L's is $4,000. High-deductible Plan F and Plan G require you to pay $2,950 in 2026 before the policy pays. The chart also references a $283 Part B deductible for 2026 in the Plan K and Plan L note. The chart is https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/medigap/basics/compare-plan-benefits

The episode shows the chart and stops. It does not say Plan G is the one you should buy. It does not say Advantage is cheaper. Price depends on the county, the company, and the year. The plan finder asks for a ZIP code because of that. The ZIP code on our recording is left blank, and the narrator says to use your own.

Plans C and F are not available to every new beneficiary. The chart says eligibility depends on when you first became eligible for Medicare. The episode points at that note and does not try to restate every exception.
