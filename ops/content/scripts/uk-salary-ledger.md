## What the official page says

In the 2026 to 2027 tax year, GOV.UK states that the maximum you can save in ISAs is £20,000. That number is a ceiling on new subscriptions. It is not a suggestion that every person should deposit £20,000. Someone who takes home less than that cannot hit the ceiling, and someone who takes home more is not required to.

From 6 April 2027, GOV.UK's tax-free savings newsletter 23 says the overall annual ISA subscription limit stays at £20,000, while investors aged under 65 can subscribe up to £12,000 of that allowance to cash ISAs. People aged 65 and over keep a £20,000 cash ISA limit. The newsletter is here: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tax-free-savings-newsletter-23/tax-free-savings-newsletter-23-september-2026

Those two dates are the spine of the episode. Everything else is an illustration with the assumptions written on screen.

## Three illustrations, labeled as illustrations

Assume, for the drawing only, a workplace pension that the employer will match up to a stated percentage. The percentage on screen is an assumption, printed as "assumption," because schemes differ. Assume no high-interest debt in these drawings. If high-interest debt exists, this episode does not rank an ISA above it.

Illustration A. Take-home pay is below the cost of a modest monthly budget that the video also prints as an assumption. The drawing shows the ISA line at zero and the pension line at whatever the employer match requires to avoid leaving the match unclaimed. The lesson is the match, not a fund.

Illustration B. Take-home pay covers the budget assumption and leaves a surplus smaller than £20,000 a year. The drawing splits the surplus into cash for a named emergency-fund target, again an assumption, and a remainder that could go to a stocks and shares ISA. The video does not name a fund, a platform, or an expected return.

Illustration C. The surplus is larger than £20,000. The drawing stops at the ceiling. Money above the ceiling is outside the ISA. The video says only that the ceiling does not move with the salary.

## What changes in April 2027, and what does not

For someone under 65, the cash portion of the allowance is the part that narrows. The overall ceiling in the newsletter stays £20,000. A person who wanted the entire allowance in cash will not be able to do that after the change. A person who already uses a stocks and shares ISA for part of the allowance is looking at a different constraint. The video reads the newsletter sentence rather than paraphrasing it from memory.

## How the episode stays inside the rules

Each salary drawing uses a different surplus, so the videos in this series are not the same slide with a new title. The on-screen source is the GOV.UK page, paused long enough to read the sentence about the £20,000 maximum. Comments that ask "what should I buy" are answered with the same boundary: read your scheme, read the allowance page, and treat the drawings as drawings.
